# Changelog

All notable changes to the LibKa Design Platform are listed here, newest first. Version numbers have the form major.minor.patch, as in [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/); releases within 2.0 bring new features as well as fixes, and a change that can break a tool built on LibKa is marked as breaking in its entry.

## 2.0.4 (2026-11-15): themes and brands in every output, LibKa Studio's Design tab and brand editor, five templates, new talk and chart blocks

- **A new block, `:::agenda`:** the plan of a talk, built from the `##` sections and parts (and the sections that count as `##`: releases, decisions, the revision history), never the notes or the references; its title is "Agenda" (in Czech "Program") unless its label names another. On slides it has a slide of its own where it stands and comes back before each section after it but the one right after it, with the current section in bold and the word "Now" (", you are here" for screen readers); the flag `once` keeps only the first, and a long agenda continues on the next slide. On the web page, in the PDF and in the Word file it is a short list of the `##` sections where it stands, which replaces the automatic contents, as `:::toc` does. It stands at the top level, once per document, and holds no content of its own.
- **A new block, `:::closing`:** the last section of a talk, a training or a proposal: the thanks as its title ("Thank you", in Czech "Děkujeme", unless the label says otherwise), at most one sentence, a recap of up to five points, and the contacts `email` and `url`, checked and written out in full as links (`:::closing[Thank you]{email=team@example.org url=https://example.org/contact}`). Only speaker notes and `:::references` may follow it; anything else is reported (`closing-not-last`) with a fix that moves the closing to the end. On the web page and in the PDF it is a section with its title as a `##` heading, listed in the contents but never in an agenda; on slides it is the closing slide, its title large; in the Word file a Heading 1 with the contacts as links.
- **Changed: `:::chart` draws horizontal and stacked bars.** `type=hbar` draws a row per category, its name on the left broken into lines and each value at its bar's end. The flag `stacked` (for `bar` and `hbar`) puts the series into one bar per category with the total in bold at its end, and shows a part's value inside it only where it fits, on a small chip of the page's colour so that it reads whatever the pattern beneath. The summary names the kind ("Stacked horizontal bar chart") and the highest and lowest totals, and the data table, on the page and in the Word file, adds a Total column. `stacked` with `line` or `donut`, or with one series, is reported (`chart-stacked`) with a fix. A bar chart whose category names would overlap, or with more than 12 categories, gets a hint on its line to use `type=hbar`.
- **A new block, `:::kpi`:** one tracked figure with its change and trend, such as `:::kpi[Median waiting time]{value="3.2 h" change="-1.1 h" since="Q2" good=down trend="5.1,4.6,4.3,3.2"}`, with at most one sentence inside. The change is an arrow and words ("up 6 pp since Q2"; in Czech "nárůst o 6 p. b. oproti Q2") with "better" or "worse" in bold, shown in green or red only together with that word; up is better unless `good=down` (costs, errors, waiting times). The trend (3 to 24 values) is a small line with its last point marked, hidden from screen readers, with a sentence that says what it shows ("Trend over 4 periods: from 81 to 94, highest 94, lowest 81.") and the values under "Show the data". Two to four `kpi` blocks in a row form one group: four side by side where there is room (a page 1024 px wide or wider), else two by two (as in the A4 PDF), else one under another (a phone); three side by side or one under another; a card is never left alone on a line. A fifth figure starts a new row and is reported (`kpi-row`); a single `kpi` stands large. A figure stays on one line and shrinks to fit its card. On slides a row shares one slide; in the Word file each figure is its name, the figure large and bold, and the change and the trend in words with the values. Malformed values are reported (`kpi-label`, `kpi-content`, `kpi-change`, `kpi-trend`).
- **Themes, the front matter key `theme`:** a theme sets the type, the heading weight, the corners and the density. Two are built in: `classic` (the default and the look until now: Fraunces headings over Inter, semi-bold headings, medium corners) and `modern` (Space Grotesk headings over Inter, bold headings, small corners). A `.theme.json` file in the project (JSON with its schema, `theme-1.schema.json`) chooses from the same settings: the fonts, a standard or large type scale, the heading weight, a comfortable or compact density and the corners. A misspelt name is reported (`theme-unknown`) with the nearest built-in theme as a fix. In a Marp deck (`marp: true`) `theme` stays Marp's, and the `marp-file` fix removes it.
- **Brands, `_brand.yml` and the front matter key `brand`:** a brand is a `_brand.yml` file in the project, in the subset of Posit's brand.yml format that LibKa reads: the name, the logo (SVG or PNG, with its alt text, and a variant for the dark modes), the primary, secondary and link colours (`typography.link.color`), the heading and body fonts, and under `defaults.libka` the project's theme, up to six chart colours, greys tinted with the primary colour's hue (`neutral: tinted`) and an `id`. Without `brand:` the build takes the `_brand.yml` next to the document or in a folder above it, up to the project's root, and the theme it names; a document's `brand:` (a path relative to the document, or `libka` for LibKa's own) and `theme:` win. An address, an absolute path or a file outside the project is refused. Every key LibKa does not read is reported: a key brand.yml does not have is an error with the nearest key, one LibKa does not use a warning that says why; 35 codes for brand and theme files are listed with the problem codes.
- **Brand colours are solved to pass contrast:** each colour is a seed. A role takes the brand's colour where it passes, else the nearest shade of the same hue that does, in each of the four modes (light, dark and both high-contrast modes), against the same thresholds as LibKa's own palette with a small margin; bars, borders and charts need 3:1, links and accent text 4.5:1. The build says what it did, in words: "the primary colour #F2C500: bars, borders and charts use #A88800 to reach 3:1; links and accent text use #7D6400 to reach 4.5:1". Status colours, text colours and backgrounds stay LibKa's, unless the greys are tinted. The first build writes the solved design as a lock next to the brand file (`<id>.<theme>.design.lock.json`, to commit with it) and writes it again when the brand, the theme or LibKa's solver changes; every signed manifest now records the design (the brand's and the theme's hashes, the solver and the lock's hash).
- **Fonts:** LibKa ships eight font families and never fetches a font. Headings: Fraunces, Inter, Literata, Bitter, Space Grotesk, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next or Public Sans; body text: Inter, Literata, Bitter, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next or Public Sans; code is always JetBrains Mono. A brand's font outside the list is an error that names the fonts it can use (`brand-font-unknown`). A page loads only the font files it uses, and a build from the npm package copies only its design's fonts with their licences.
- **Themes and brands in every output:** the web page, the PDF and the slides take the design's colours, fonts and corners; the logo stands above the title of the web page and the PDF's first page and on the title slide, with its alt text, the dark variant in the dark modes. The Word file takes the design's colours, the Office fonts in place of the design's (Georgia for Fraunces, Literata and Bitter, Arial for the sans families), a large scale's reading sizes and the logo above the title; its charts and diagrams are drawn in the design, and its conformance report says what Word cannot take (the fonts, rounded corners, the dark and high-contrast modes). Chart series follow the brand: `defaults.libka.charts`, else the primary and secondary colours, then LibKa's series, each moved to 3:1 (4.5:1 in high contrast) in each mode where needed; the build names the series that moved, those LibKa filled and the brand's colours that are much alike. A brand does not carry a logo on every page, the logo's clear space, the secondary colour beyond the AI marks and the charts, heading weights, link decoration (links stay underlined) or backgrounds.
- **Changed (breaking for tools that read the token resolver): the modifier `theme` in `libka.resolver.json` is now `mode`,** with the same values (`light`, the default, `dark`, `light-high-contrast`, `dark-high-contrast`), since a theme is now a document's design style. A tool that resolved the tokens with `theme` passes `mode` instead; the AI generation profiles (`governance/a2-profiles.json`) name it `mode` too. In CSS the mode is still the attribute `data-theme` with `data-contrast="high"`, as before.
- **Changed: LibKa's six chart series are retuned per mode.** They keep their hues and order and take a lightness per mode (light, dark and both high-contrast modes), so that each passes 3:1 (4.5:1 in high contrast) on every background, and the token build checks that they stay told apart under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia in every mode (CIE76 ΔE 15 or more). Their names are now Aqua, Iris, Apricot, Forest, Coral and Slate, and the light values are `#138DAD`, `#817AFE`, `#C67A1D`, `#206424`, `#DA5353` and `#8B8C8C`. Every line, line marker and legend line lies on a casing in the text colour, so its shape passes 3:1 whatever the series colour, as bars already did by their outline, and the bars' patterns are drawn in the surface colour.
- **Changed: italic body text is a real italic.** Inter and the other body families ship with their italic fonts, so italics on the web page, on the slides and in the PDF are no longer a slanted upright.
- **Changed: the Word file's theme colours are LibKa's.** The twelve colours Word offers in its theme palette are the design's text shades that pass contrast, in a theme named LibKa, in place of Office's own; this applies to LibKa's own design too.
- **Changed: Guard checks the slides' dark mode and text over images.** A deck is now measured in its dark mode in both contrast levels as well as in light, and a failure stops the signing, as in light. Text over an image or a gradient inside a document's content (a LibKa block, the document's header, a slide) is now a contrast failure, since its contrast cannot be measured and text in a LibKa document stays on a plain colour; in a site's frame around the document it is still skipped.
- **Changed: Guard checks more of the Word file.** Alt text is checked in the headers, the footers and the notes too, and the new `docx-theme-color` fails any text or shading that takes a theme colour (`w:themeColor`, `w:themeFill`), which Word shows in place of the colour written beside it (WCAG 1.4.3).
- **Fixed: a line chart's value labels no longer sit on other series' markers.** Each label goes above its point, else below it, else beside it, only where it touches no marker, no line and no other label and stays inside the plot; a value with no room is left out of the drawing and stays in the summary and the table.
- **Fixed: an empty table cell no longer fails PDF/UA.** Chrome leaves an empty cell out of the PDF's structure, so a row was a column short (7.2-43); the PDF repair puts an empty cell back under its column's header. An empty corner above the row headers (7.2-42) is put back the same way.
- **Fixed: a long author-date citation wraps.** A citation with a long organisation's name could not break and failed reflow at 320 CSS pixels; the year stays with the name before it.
- **Fixed: Czech dates and percentage points stay on one line:** "31. 12. 2025", "1. 4." and "6 p. b." no longer break inside.
- **Changed: `libka build` and `libka lint` say which design they use.** They name the brand and the theme and where each came from ("the brand in _brand.yml (the project's, found by itself) with the Modern theme (from defaults.libka.theme in _brand.yml)"), what the brand's colours became, which chart series LibKa filled, the fonts and what a Word file uses in their place, and the logo and where it goes, each on a `libka:` line. `libka lint` checks the brand and theme files with the document; an error in them stops the build.
- **Changed (breaking for MCP clients that read Guard's PDF or Word results under `web`, or the slides' PDF under `slides`): the MCP server knows designs and templates.** `libka_check` takes an optional `path`, so the document's brand and theme files are found and checked, and returns the design. `libka_build` returns `notes` (the design in words, a signing key made, a PDF not validated) and Guard's results per output (`web`, `pdf`, `docx`, `slides`, `slidesPdf`), which were all under `web` and `slides`. The templates are resources: `libka://templates` lists them and `libka://template/{id}/{lang}` gives one in English or Czech.
- **Changed: the AI skill writes the new blocks, brands and templates.** The skill names `agenda`, `closing`, `kpi` and the horizontal and stacked bars and when to use them, and the block reference (also `libka://reference`) has a "Brand file" section: a full example, the keys LibKa reads and what each colour drives, the fonts, the logo, the chart colours, the lock and what a brand does not carry. The skill writes a theme or a brand only when the user names one, never colours or fonts in a document; it writes a brand as `_brand.yml` at the project's root and tells the user what the brand could not carry; and it starts from a template where one fits.
- **Templates, and `create-libka --template`:** five templates in English and Czech, each for an invented support team and with a comment at its top on how to use it: `report` (a summary, key figures, a chart, a decision and next steps; web, PDF and Word), `talk` (an agenda, key figures, a statement, speaker notes and a closing slide with contacts; the Modern theme; web, PDF and slides), `manual` (steps, callouts, do and don't, a glossary and a checklist; web, PDF, slides for training and Word), `policy` (its owner and review dates, numbered rules, a table of retention periods and a revision history; web, PDF and Word) and `decision-record` (decisions with their context, the options in a comparison and the consequences, and a proposal still to decide; web, PDF and Word). Each names its theme, never a brand, which the project's `_brand.yml` gives. `npm create libka@latest my-docs -- --template report` (or `-t`) starts a project from one, and the interactive start asks for one; the example stays the default. The template becomes `docs/<file>.md`, and the project's scripts build, check and verify it with its outputs. The starter's README, in English and Czech, says how to give a project its look and brand.
- **Changed: the README** opens its LibKa Studio section with what the Studio is for: it opens any Markdown, shows an AI's blocks as the page they make, and lets a person edit them visually, the blocks nobody edited kept byte for byte. It then names the modes Write, Blocks and Focus, the block bar, Checks and Present; "AI authoring" links to it and lists the forms of the `ai` key; and it lists the five templates.
- **New in LibKa Studio: documents in their design.** The preview, the fitted slides, Present, Guard and the exports use the document's theme and brand, chosen as `libka build` chooses them: `theme:` and `brand:`, else a `_brand.yml` beside the document or in a folder above it. The brand's files (`_brand.yml` and its logos) are the document's files, like its images: kept in the browser, read from an opened folder or a .zip, written back to a linked folder in place (a project's `_brand.yml` above the document too) and put in the .zip, so an export holds what `libka build` reads. A brand or theme file with an error, or a missing one, leaves LibKa's design, with the error in Checks on the front matter's line; its warnings are Advice. Guard in the Studio measures the slides' dark mode in both contrast levels too, as the CLI does. Every font family works offline.
- **New in LibKa Studio: the Design tab** ("Vzhled" in Czech), beside Preview, Checks and Properties: the themes Classic and Modern as cards with a sample in their fonts; the brand, LibKa's own, the document's or its project's, or another document's from the library, whose brand files are then copied to this document; the solver's result in words, in English or Czech; and a link to Checks when the files have problems. It writes `theme:` and `brand:` (`brand: libka` sets a project's brand aside) as one step to undo.
- **New in LibKa Studio: the brand editor.** "Edit the brand…" (or "Create a brand…") in the Design tab opens a dialog: the name; the primary, secondary and link colours and up to six chart colours, each a hex field with the colour picker beside it (a wrong value is marked, with its error in words, and never saved); the heading and text fonts from LibKa's list or the theme's; greys tinted with the primary colour's hue; and the logo and its dark variant (SVG or PNG) with alt text. Beside the controls, the four modes as the outputs show them and the solver's words follow every change. Each valid change is written to the document's `_brand.yml` at once, changing only what changed (comments, order and other keys stay as written), and the preview follows. **Reset** goes back to the brand as it was when the dialog opened; **Remove the brand**, pressed twice, deletes the brand file, and the document goes back to LibKa's design (Versions keep the text, not the brand file). The dialog opens at its heading, in two columns from 760 px and one on a phone.
- **New in LibKa Studio: New document starts from a template.** It opens a picker: Blank first and chosen, so Enter makes a blank document as before, then the five templates with a picture, a name and a description in the interface's language; Create, Cancel or Escape. A template opens in the interface's language.
- **Changed in LibKa Studio: a dialog keeps its heading and its buttons in view.** A dialog with a heading (Preferences, Keyboard shortcuts, the brand editor, New document, the accessibility statement, About, a version compared, the questions the Studio asks) has a close button beside its heading and its buttons at the bottom, both in view; only the content between them scrolls, inside the rounded frame, so no corner covers a scroll bar and Close is never scrolled away. Preferences says that each change applies and is kept at once; the accessibility statement opens on its text, which scrolls with the keyboard; in a window too low for a fixed heading and buttons (a phone on its side, a large zoom) the whole dialog scrolls; Preferences no longer scrolls sideways at 320 CSS pixels (the command keys put Change under the keys where the row is narrow). Escape still closes a dialog.
- **Changed in LibKa Studio: on a phone, Properties in the block bar is an icon** (sliders, named "Properties" for screen readers and in its tooltip), so a figure's bar fits one row on a 390 px phone; the bars on a desktop and in Focus keep the word.
- **Fixed in LibKa Studio: Guard checks a deck or a page as built.** A deck starts as its reader last left it (D for dark, C for contrast, the letter shortcuts on or off), and a page with the tab its reader last chose; the Studio's checking frames read those stored choices too, so after a deck was used in Present, Guard in the Studio could measure the dark colours as the light page, the standard colours against 7:1, or a deck without its key hints, and report findings the CLI does not, or miss ones it does. The checking frames also followed the system's request for more contrast and its forced colours (a Windows contrast theme), which the CLI's Chrome never has, and so did the charts, diagrams and UI specimens the Studio draws for a Word file. They now start with empty storage of their own, without the system's request for more contrast (except in the more-contrast passes, as in the CLI) and without forced colours, as the CLI's fresh Chrome does.
- **Fixed in LibKa Studio: an open menu keeps clear of the controls under it when they move.** It worked out its place only as it opened, so when a control under it moved afterwards (the editor's toolbar settling) it could leave a sliver smaller than 24 px (WCAG 2.5.8); an open menu now measures its place again while it stays open.
- **Security: supported versions.** `SECURITY.md` has a table of supported versions: the latest release gets security fixes, and when a new minor version is released, the one before it still gets them for three months.
- **Security: a software bill of materials.** Each release on GitHub carries an SBOM for each of its npm packages, in CycloneDX JSON (`<package>-<version>.cdx.json`), with every dependency's version, licence and integrity hash as npm installs it; the release's `SHA256SUMS` lists them.
- **Dependencies:** no third-party package changed its version, and none was added to the npm packages or LibKa Studio; the repository's tests and token build gained the development dependencies culori (with @types/culori) and fast-check. New in `fonts/`: Literata, Bitter, Space Grotesk, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Public Sans, and the italics of Inter, from Fontsource 5.3.0, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

## 2.0.3 (2026-11-09): AI provenance, seven new blocks, a new LibKa Studio layout

- **AI provenance, the front matter key `ai` and the attribute `ai=`:** a document says how AI took part, `ai: ai-assisted` (AI helped with a person's text) or `ai: ai-generated` (AI wrote it); the long form puts the use under `use` and adds, optionally, `reviewed-by` (who checked it and holds editorial responsibility), `approved-by`, `tool`, `date`, `checked` and `label` beside it (`ai:`, then `use: ai-generated` and `reviewed-by: Ann Novak` on the lines under it, indented); a block marks the parts AI wrote with `ai=generated` or `ai=assisted`. It is the author's declaration, recorded in the signed manifest (`provenance`, and each marked block in `ai`). A visible label is shown only when the author turns it on (`label: true`): one sentence under the title on the web page, in the PDF, in the Word file and on the title slide ("Created with AI tools. Reviewed and approved by …"), and an "AI-generated" badge before each marked block nobody is named as having reviewed. Nothing is blocked: `lint` reminds the author when AI-generated content names no reviewer and shows no label, since AI-generated text that informs the public on matters of public interest needs one or the other (EU AI Act, Article 50(4)), and only the author can tell whether a document does. The skill and the MCP server mark what AI writes (never with an invented reviewer); LibKa Studio has the fields in the document's properties. Malformed values are reported (`ai-invalid`).
- **Changed: AI summaries follow the same rules.** `:::summary{source=ai}` counts as `ai=generated`. A missing `reviewed-by` is a reminder, no longer a problem (`summary-reviewer` is gone), and the summary's "AI-assisted, reviewed by …" line is shown only when the document turns the AI label on, now as "AI-generated" with "Reviewed and approved by …". The reviewer is always recorded in the manifest.
- **A new block, `:::byline`, and the front matter key `author`:** one or more people, each with an optional role (`author: Ann Novak`, or a list with `name` and `role`). The authors appear under the title of the web page, the PDF and the Word file and on the title slide of a deck; `:::byline` shows them where it stands instead. Their names are also the page's author metadata, the PDF's author (in its XMP and document information) and the Word file's author, and the signed manifest lists them as declared. LibKa Studio edits them in the document's properties.
- **A new block, `:::download`:** a link to a file named by what it is, with its format, size and, for a PDF, its pages in the link text (`Annual report 2025 (PDF, 2.1 MB, 48 pages)`), and an optional sentence about it. For a file next to the document, `libka build` works out the size and a PDF's pages and copies the file into the output folder, as it does for images; a missing file is reported on its line. The format comes from the file name unless `type` names it; a file on another site needs `type` and `size`. Sizes and page counts are written in the document's language (`2,1 MB, 3 strany`).
- **A new block, `:::revisions`:** a document's revision history as a Markdown table with the columns version, date and change, and optionally author and approved by, recognised by their headings in English or Czech (`Verze`, `Datum`, `Změna`, `Autor`, `Schválil`, `Schválila` or `Schválení`). Dates are checked (YYYY-MM-DD), the same version twice is reported, and the rows must run newest first or oldest first by date and version. It is a section of its own, like the reference list: a level-2 heading with an anchor ("Revision history" unless its label names it), listed in the contents, over the table on the web page, in the PDF and the Word file (Heading 1), and a slide of its own that continues on the next one when the table is long. A heading of the author's own right above it is reported (`revisions-heading`). The signed manifest lists each version with its date and who approved it, as declared.
- **A new block, `::::decision`:** a decision record as a section of its own. Its title states the decision; `status` (proposed, accepted, rejected, superseded) is shown as a word on a badge, with `date` and who decided (`by`), both required once a decision is taken. A sentence stating the decision may come first, then `:::context` (required), `:::options` (which may hold a `:::comparison`) and `:::consequences`, always shown in that order under their own subheadings. Like a release, each decision is a level-2 heading with an anchor, listed in the contents and its own slide; in the Word file it is Heading 1 with each part as Heading 2.
- **A new block, `::::pros-cons`:** the pros and cons of one option. The label names the option; `:::pros` and `:::cons` each hold one bulleted list, shown pros first under the written words "Pros" and "Cons" (in Czech "Výhody" and "Nevýhody"), side by side where there is room and stacked on a phone. Both sides are required. Several options are several blocks, and a decision's `:::options` may hold one per option. In the Word file each side is a shaded panel with its word and a bulleted list.
- **A new block, `:::flow`:** how a process runs, as 2 to 6 numbered stages in a row joined by arrows drawn in CSS (stacked with arrows down on a phone), one list item per stage. `shape=cycle` marks a process that starts again, and the return to the first stage is written out under the stages ("Then it starts again: …"; in Czech „Pak vše začíná znovu: …“), never shown by an arrow alone. In the Word file it is the process's name and a numbered list. Instructions the reader carries out stay `:::steps`.
- **A new block, `::::matrix`:** points sorted into four quadrants, read left to right, top row first. `type=swot` fills in the quadrants' names (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats; in Czech "Silné stránky", "Slabé stránky", "Příležitosti", "Hrozby"); `type=grid` names each `:::q` and says in words what changes across (`x=`) and down (`y=`). Each quadrant is a group named by its visible name; the axes are written beside the grid and read before it. A 2 × 2 grid on the web page, in the PDF and on slides, stacked on a phone; in the Word file a 2 × 2 table under a header row with the title.
- **Changed: dates are written in the document's language, and the new front matter key `date-format` chooses how.** The source keeps YYYY-MM-DD; the web page, the PDF, the slides and the Word file write every date (under the title, the review dates, a decision, a release, the revision history and a timeline's days and months) as `long` "28 October 2026" / "28. října 2026" (the default), `short` "28/10/2026" / "28. 10. 2026" or `iso` "2026-10-28", the way they were written before. The `<time datetime>` and the manifest keep ISO. A misspelt value is reported (`date-format-unknown`) with the nearest one as a fix; the Studio has the key among the document's fields.
- **Fixed: a key figure in `:::metrics` no longer breaks inside.** On a phone "184,000" broke into "184,0" and "00", and on a wide page a four-figure row broke "1,234,567". The columns are now at least as wide as the block's longest figure, and the figures shrink to fit their tiles (down to 1.75rem before the tiles stack); a figure breaks only at a space, and its digit groups stay together in every language ("184 000").
- **Changed: `libka build` leaves only this build's outputs.** It removes the document's own outputs from an earlier build that it did not make again (the slides, say, after a build without `deck`) and says so on a "removed" line; nothing else in the output folder is touched. Its output heads the web page's checks "web:", as it heads the slides' "deck:", lists the conformance reports among the files written, and shows the PDF/UA-2 result, which is recorded and never required. The MCP server's `libka_build` returns the conformance reports and the removed files too.
- **Changed: the conformance report (`.conformance.md`) is written in the document's language**, English or Czech. The WCAG criteria keep their English names, since there is no official Czech translation of WCAG 2.
- **Changed: the automatic contents come after the opening text**, right before the first `##` section, on the web page and in the Word file; they stood above it. The PDF keeps its bookmarks instead.
- **Changed: the Word file no longer ends with "Diagram sources".** A diagram is its picture and its description; the Mermaid source stays in the LibKa Markdown file and on the web page.
- **Fixed: the PDF's last page no longer holds only the footer line.** The end of the content moves to the last page with it.
- **Fixed in LibKa Studio: text such as "16:9" stays as written in the visual editor.** It showed as "16" and a ":9" chip, and "a:b" was saved as `a\:b`. Only the inline directives LibKa knows are read as directives now, as in the build, and typed text is escaped only where it would become one of them.
- **Fixed in LibKa Studio: Bold, Italic, Code and Strikethrough show as on only when all of the selected text has them.** A paragraph with a little code in it showed Code as on. A click (or Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+E, Ctrl+Shift+X) on a partly marked selection now marks all of it, and a second click removes the mark.
- **Fixed in LibKa Studio: Preferences and the accessibility statement open at their top**, with the focus on their heading; they opened scrolled to the Close button at the end.

## 2.0.0 (2026-10-11): the first public release

LibKa was developed in a private repository up to 2.0; this repository starts with it.

- **Documents:** one Markdown file with LibKa blocks (thirty-five block types at this release, from headings and lists to charts, diagrams and timelines) builds a web page, an A4 PDF, 16:9 slides and an editable Word file; each document chooses its outputs.
- **Guard:** every output is checked (axe-core WCAG 2.2 A/AA in Chrome, text contrast 4.5:1 and 7:1 in high contrast, structure rules, reflow at 320 CSS pixels, slide fit; veraPDF PDF/UA-1 for the PDF; the Word file's structure) and the outputs that pass are signed; a conformance report maps each WCAG 2.2 criterion to EN 301 549. A PDF built without veraPDF is marked as not validated in its manifest, its conformance report and `libka verify`.
- **LibKa Studio:** an installable, local-first web app with visual and source editing, live checks with quick fixes, versions, English and Czech; self-audited against WCAG 2.2 A/AA, with the evidence in `docs/acr/studio.md`. Help, About LibKa Studio gives the licence, the third-party notices and what happens to your documents; `build.mjs --hosted` builds a copy for a web address of its own, whose headers keep a host's statistics script out (`no-transform`); it starts offline on hosts that redirect `index.html` too.
- **Documentation:** the documents in `docs/` are LibKa documents themselves, from [getting started](docs/getting-started.md) to the conformance reports; the block registry carries a Czech example for every block. HTML comments are allowed in documents as notes for editors and are never rendered.
- **Design tokens:** W3C DTCG 2025.10 tokens with light, dark and high-contrast themes, generated into CSS, SCSS and Tailwind themes with contrast gates.
- **AI authoring:** an Agent Skill and an MCP server; AI-written content is checked like any other, and content the document declares as AI-written is listed in the signed manifest. For Claude Code both come as one plugin from this repository's marketplace file; for Claude Desktop the server comes as an extension (`.mcpb`), and the skill as a `.zip` for claude.ai and Desktop ([`docs/ai-tools.md`](docs/ai-tools.md)).
- **Packages:** `@libka/cli` (the `libka` command, with LibKa Studio), `@libka/mcp`, `@libka/tokens`, and `npm create libka` for a new project with an example in English or Czech, npm scripts and a CI workflow. `libka doctor` says what a computer has and what works without each missing piece; a wrong command, a missing value or a missing file is named, and a misspelled front-matter key gets the nearest known one. Each project signs with its own key, and trusts its first key in its own list.
- **Releases:** built from a tag by `publish.yml`: the npm packages with provenance (this first version's packages are published by hand, so they carry no provenance statement), the MCP server in the MCP Registry, and a draft GitHub Release with the Desktop extension, the skill `.zip` and SHA-256 checksums, published after review. Run by hand, the workflow is a dry run by default.
